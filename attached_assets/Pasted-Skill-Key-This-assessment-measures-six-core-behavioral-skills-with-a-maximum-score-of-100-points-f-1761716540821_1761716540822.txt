Skill Key
This assessment measures six core behavioral skills, with a maximum score of 100 points for each:
•	HA: High Agency
•	IC: Intellectual Curiosity
•	RA: Resilience
•	EE: Empathy & Ethics
•	GM: Growth Mindset
•	IA: Interpersonal Agility

The 15 Assessment Questions and Options
For each scenario, select the option (A, B, C, or D) that best describes your likely action.
1. Scenario: You are hosting a dinner party. A key ingredient is missing, and the stores are about to close.
•	A. HA: Quickly change the recipe to use only what's on hand and start cooking immediately, focusing on saving time.
•	B. IC: Call a foodie friend to find the most interesting, surprising thing you could use to replace the missing ingredient.
•	C. GM: Take a few minutes to quickly look up advanced techniques for making the dish work with fewer ingredients, viewing it as a new skill challenge.
•	D. IA: Send a quick text to guests to see if one is passing a store and can grab the item, offering to pay them back.
2. Scenario: A friend tells you they failed their driving test for the third time and are deeply upset.
•	A. HA: Insist on driving practice right away, identifying their key mistakes and personally coaching them until the flaw is fixed.
•	B. RA: Remind them that failure is normal and encourage them to take a short break before getting back to their routine.
•	C. EE: Focus entirely on validating their frustration and offer to listen as long as they need, avoiding judgment or practical advice.
•	D. IA: Suggest setting up practice sessions with several different, patient friends to get varied feedback and make the process more fun.
3. Scenario: You are trying to repair a small, complicated piece of antique furniture, and the original instructions are in an unfamiliar language.
•	A. IC: Focus time on translating the original documents and researching the specific construction methods used during that time period.
•	B. RA: Accept that it will take longer, and start working on the simpler parts first, slowly tackling the hardest piece last.
•	C. EE: Find a highly specialized artisan in the field and offer them a fair price just to explain the key technique needed for the fix.
•	D. GM: Enrol in a weekend class on that specific period of furniture restoration, seeing it as a long-term investment.
4. Scenario: You plan a vacation with three friends, but two of them suddenly refuse to pay the agreed-upon deposit, threatening the whole trip.
•	A. HA: Pay the full deposit yourself immediately, then deal with the non-paying friends privately to secure their commitment and money later.
•	B. IC: Analyse the detailed cancellation rules and timeline for the trip to understand exactly what options are available if the friends drop out completely.
•	C. RA: Politely tell the travel agent that the trip might need to be delayed or cancelled, focusing on protecting your own financial stability first.
•	D. EE: Tell the travel agent to move forward with the trip for the two paying friends only, focusing on honouring their commitment.
5. Scenario: You see an older person at a bus stop clearly struggling to carry several heavy grocery bags.
•	A. IC: Step in and ask which bags are heaviest and most awkwardly shaped so you can choose the ones that offer the most relief.
•	B. RA: Offer to help carry the bags and stay with the person until the bus arrives, protecting them from the stress of being alone with the load.
•	C. EE: Offer to carry the bags all the way to their final destination, as this provides the maximum amount of support.
•	D. IA: Ask two other people waiting at the stop to help, dividing the load among several individuals to make it lighter for everyone.
6. Scenario: You have been running a personal blog/creative project for years, and a long-time reader posts a detailed, hateful critique of your recent work.
•	A. HA: Write and publish a point-by-point factual response that defends your creative choices and corrects any misinterpretations in the critique.
•	B. GM: Take the negative critique as a challenge and immediately start seeking mentorship or courses to improve the specific flaws mentioned.
•	C. IA: Reach out privately to several other readers to find out if the critique has merit and coordinate a group response to the public comment.
•	D. RA: Delete the hateful comment and decide to continue working on your project exactly as before, focusing on the joy of creating.
7. Scenario: A close friend asks you to co-sign a lease or loan for a business idea you believe is fundamentally flawed and risky.
•	A. IC: Research the legal requirements of co-signing and the friend's financial history to gather objective data on the exact risks involved.
•	B. RA: Politely refuse the request, citing your own current financial stress and the need to protect your future stability.
•	C. GM: Agree to help the friend, but only on the condition that they complete a business course and secure a mentor before you sign.
•	D. EE: Tell the friend you cannot co-sign, but immediately offer them a smaller, non-repayable loan to help with initial start-up costs.
8. Scenario: You discover your local volunteer group's election process is accidentally confusing, leading to only wealthy, older members being elected.
•	A. HA: Immediately draft and circulate a new, simpler election form and host a pop-up station to help every member register correctly.
•	B. IC: Spend time analysing the old form and historical voter data to pinpoint exactly which sections created the subtle barrier to entry.
•	C. EE: Offer dedicated time to reach out to younger, less wealthy members and ensure they are personally guided through the registration process.
•	D. GM: Propose a rule change that mandates training for all potential candidates, ensuring everyone starts with the same knowledge base.
9. Scenario: You are watching a live event a concert when a drunk person starts loudly heckling the person next to you.
•	A. RA: Remain quiet and adjust your attention to ignore the person, waiting patiently for the event staff to handle the issue.
•	B. EE: Speak to the heckler in a calm, low voice, expressing concern for their behaviour’s impact on everyone else's experience.
•	C. IA: Discreetly alert the event staff and point out the heckler, allowing the professionals to manage the conflict.
•	D. HA: Directly ask the heckler to leave the area, telling them their behaviour is disrupting the entire section.
10. Scenario: A sudden, major repair of the roof occurs in your house, demanding immediate attention and a large expense.
•	A. HA: Start tearing out the damaged ceiling/walls yourself to stop the water and isolate the problem before calling a professional.
•	B. IC: Research temporary fixes and the science of water damage and mold growth to ensure any quick repair avoids long-term damage.
•	C. GM: Immediately seek out online courses or videos on home repair to learn the necessary skill, guaranteeing you can handle similar future problems.
•	D. RA: Take a moment to calm down, then call three highly rated professionals to get quotes, ensuring you don't panic-hire the wrong service.
11. Scenario: Your close-knit group of friends has developed an inside joke or ritual that consistently leaves one specific member feeling excluded.
•	A. HA: Immediately confront the main instigator privately, demanding that they stop the joke/ritual for the sake of the group dynamic.
•	B. IC: Observe the group interactions closely for a week, logging exactly when the exclusion happens to determine its root cause.
•	C. EE: Stop participating in the joke/ritual yourself and ensure the excluded friend is included in other, separate activities you plan.
•	D. IA: Initiate a casual conversation with the entire group, suggesting a completely new, shared activity or joke that everyone can enjoy equally.
12. Scenario: You need to organize a huge, complex family reunion in a few weeks, and you have no clear plan or starting budget.
•	A. RA: Spend the first day breaking the event into 100 small, dependent tasks (invites, menu, travel) to create a concrete, manageable checklist.
•	B. EE: Communicate clearly to the main family contact that the time frame is impossible and propose a revised, achievable schedule for next year.
•	C. GM: Find an event planner or experienced family member who has successfully done this before and ask for their advice on how to structure the starting phase.
•	D. IA: Identify two capable family members and delegate the non-critical initial tasks (like creating the guest list) while defining the core budget.
13. Scenario: You are stuck on a difficult personal challenge and need a solution quickly.
•	A. HA: Immediately order specialized tools or hire an expert to solve the problem, bypassing the need to learn the detailed subject matter.
•	B. IC: Spend focused time reading technical guides and forums related to the problem until discovering a unique solution.
•	C. RA: Accept the difficulty for now and deal with the minor inconvenience until a simpler solution or external aid appears later.
•	D. GM: Enrol in a targeted course to learn the skill needed, ensuring this kind of problem can be solved for good.
14. Scenario: You receive harsh, unfair criticism online from a public figure about a personal creative project.
•	A. RA: Write a calm, factual response that addresses only the verifiable points, completely avoiding emotional defence.
•	B. EE: Send a kind, professional message stating that while respecting their platform, the harsh tone prevents constructive discussion.
•	C. GM: Seek coaching on public relations and crisis communication to learn how to deal with aggressive public figures effectively.
•	D. IA: Talk to trusted peers and ask them to act as a buffer for future comments or to take over the public-facing response.
15. Scenario: You are tasked with defining a new household budget but there are multiple strong, conflicting opinions from family members.
•	A. HA: Implement a budget you deem most effective, then proactively track data to show everyone that your method is superior.
•	B. IC: Talk to all family members, focusing on the reasons behind their opinions to synthesize a completely new, hybrid solution.
•	C. EE: Focus on designing a routine that equally minimizes the workload and maximizes the personal time for the two most critical people involved.
•	D. IA: Talk to all family and lead them through a structured exercise to build consensus on the way forward.

Scoring Key and Calculation Guide
The correct letter choice for each question is mapped to a single skill. Award 20 points for every correct answer for that skill.
•	HA (High Agency) is the correct choice for: Q1-A, Q2-A, Q4-A, Q8-A, Q9-D, Q10-A, Q11-A, Q15-A
•	IC (Intellectual Curiosity) is the correct choice for: Q1-B, Q3-A, Q4-B, Q5-A, Q7-A, Q8-B, Q10-B, Q11-B, Q15-B
•	RA (Resilience) is the correct choice for: Q2-B, Q3-B, Q4-C, Q6-D, Q7-B, Q9-A, Q12-A, Q13-C, Q14-A
•	EE (Empathy & Ethics) is the correct choice for: Q2-C, Q3-C, Q4-D, Q5-C, Q7-D, Q8-C, Q9-B, Q11-C, Q12-B, Q14-B, Q15-C
•	GM (Growth Mindset) is the correct choice for: Q1-C, Q3-D, Q6-B, Q7-C, Q8-D, Q10-C, Q12-C, Q13-D, Q14-C
•	IA (Interpersonal Agility) is the correct choice for: Q1-D, Q2-D, Q5-D, Q6-C, Q9-C, Q11-D, Q12-D, Q14-D, Q15-D
The maximum score for any skill is 100 points.
