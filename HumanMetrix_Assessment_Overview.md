# HumanMetrix: AI-Powered Assessment Platform
### Overview for Industrial Psychology Review

---

## What is HumanMetrix?

HumanMetrix is a behavioural assessment platform purpose-built for the AI era. It provides employers, HR teams, and talent partners with objective, standardised tools to evaluate candidates across the behavioural competencies and technical mindsets that determine success in AI-augmented roles.

Where traditional hiring relies on CV screening and unstructured interviews — both of which are heavily susceptible to bias and inconsistency — HumanMetrix delivers structured, data-driven assessments with immediate results. Every candidate is evaluated against the same criteria, under the same conditions, producing comparable outputs that support fair and defensible hiring decisions.

The platform offers four individual assessments and one team-level diagnostic. All assessments are administered online, are adaptive in format, and produce detailed PDF reports for both candidates and employers.

## Assessment Philosophy

### The Problem with Traditional Hiring

- **75% of CVs contain exaggerated claims**, making self-reported experience an unreliable signal
- Unstructured interviews introduce unconscious bias at scale, disadvantaging candidates from underrepresented groups
- Standard aptitude tests were designed for industrial-era roles and do not capture the competencies that define high performance in AI-driven environments

### The HumanMetrix Approach

HumanMetrix assessments are grounded in three principles:

1. **Behavioural measurement over self-report** — assessments present candidates with realistic scenarios and decision points rather than asking them to rate their own abilities

2. **Standardisation** — every candidate sits the same structured assessment, eliminating the variability of interviewer judgment and CV interpretation

3. **AI-era relevance** — the competency frameworks and assessment content are specifically designed to identify the traits and capabilities that predict success in roles where AI tools are central to daily work

## Individual Assessment 1: FIT Assessment

### What It Measures

The FIT Assessment measures six core behavioural traits that research associates with sustained performance, adaptability, and collaborative effectiveness. These traits are particularly predictive in complex, fast-changing environments where AI tools augment human decision-making.

| Capability | What It Reflects |
|---|---|
| **High Agency** | The tendency to take initiative, ownership, and autonomous action without requiring direction. Predicts proactive problem-solving and self-directed execution. |
| **Intellectual Curiosity** | Drive to explore, question, and learn continuously. Particularly important in AI-augmented roles where tools, methods, and knowledge evolve rapidly. |
| **Resilience** | Capacity to recover from setbacks, manage pressure, and maintain performance under uncertainty. A key predictor of retention and long-term effectiveness. |
| **Empathy & Ethics** | Sensitivity to the impact of decisions on others, combined with a strong ethical orientation. Critical for roles involving AI outputs that affect people's lives. |
| **Growth Mindset** | Belief that capabilities can be developed through effort. Predicts openness to feedback, willingness to iterate, and long-term learning agility. |
| **Interpersonal Agility** | Ability to adapt communication style, build relationships across differences, and collaborate effectively in diverse teams. |

### Format

Scenario-based, adaptive assessment. Candidates respond to realistic workplace situations and decision-making contexts. Duration: approximately 14–20 minutes.

### Outputs

- Scored profile across all six capabilities (0–100 per dimension)
- Narrative interpretation of strengths and development areas
- Role-fit comparison when a target role is specified by the employer
- Full PDF report for candidate and employer

## Individual Assessment 2: AI Readiness Assessment

### What It Measures

The AI Readiness Assessment evaluates how prepared a candidate is to work effectively in AI-augmented environments — not whether they can build AI, but whether they can work alongside it productively and critically.

The assessment covers three domains:

- **Technical Fluency** — familiarity with how AI systems work at a conceptual level; understanding of inputs, outputs, limitations, and error modes
- **Human-AI Collaboration** — ability to integrate AI tools into workflows appropriately; knowing when to rely on AI outputs and when to override them
- **Adaptive Problem-Solving** — capacity to apply existing skills and knowledge in novel situations created by AI-driven change

### Why It Matters

Organisations deploying AI tools are discovering that technical implementation is only part of the challenge. Employees who lack AI readiness struggle to use tools effectively, over-rely on AI outputs without critical evaluation, or resist adoption entirely. This assessment identifies where candidates sit on that spectrum before hiring decisions are made.

### Format

Structured question-based assessment covering scenario judgments, conceptual understanding, and applied reasoning. Duration: approximately 14–20 minutes.

### Outputs

- AI Readiness score and percentile ranking against assessed population
- Breakdown across the three domains
- Role-specific commentary when a target role is provided
- Full PDF report

## Individual Assessment 3: Prompt Architecture Assessment

### What It Measures

The Prompt Architecture Assessment evaluates a candidate's ability to communicate with and direct AI systems effectively. As AI tools become embedded in professional workflows, the ability to construct clear, structured, and purposeful instructions to AI systems is becoming a core professional competency.

The assessment measures:

- **Clarity of instruction** — ability to specify objectives unambiguously
- **Contextual framing** — understanding of how context shapes AI output quality
- **Iteration and refinement** — capacity to diagnose weak outputs and improve prompts accordingly
- **Scope management** — ability to constrain AI responses appropriately to avoid over-generation or off-target results

### Why It Matters

Prompt quality directly determines the usefulness of AI tools in professional settings. Candidates who can communicate precisely with AI systems produce better outputs, complete work faster, and integrate AI more effectively into collaborative processes. This is a measurable, trainable skill — and one that is currently invisible to traditional hiring processes.

### Format

Practical, task-based assessment presenting candidates with AI interaction scenarios. Duration: approximately 14–20 minutes.

### Outputs

- Prompt Architecture score and percentile ranking
- Qualitative commentary on approach and technique
- Role-specific analysis where applicable
- Full PDF report

## Individual Assessment 4: AIQ — Algorithmic Integrity Quotient

### What It Measures

The AIQ Assessment evaluates a candidate's ethical reasoning and judgment specifically in the context of AI systems and their outputs. As AI becomes embedded in consequential decisions — hiring, credit, healthcare, law enforcement — the ability to reason carefully about algorithmic outputs is a professional responsibility, not a niche concern.

The assessment covers:

- **Bias recognition** — ability to identify situations where AI systems may reflect or amplify existing biases in data or design
- **Uncertainty calibration** — understanding that AI outputs carry confidence levels and that acting on uncertain outputs without acknowledgment is a risk
- **Responsible deployment judgment** — capacity to evaluate whether AI tools should be used in a given context, and what safeguards are appropriate
- **Transparency and accountability** — understanding of the professional obligations that come with using AI systems in decisions that affect others
- **Failure mode awareness** — familiarity with how AI systems fail, including hallucination, distributional shift, and over-reliance patterns

### Why It Matters for Industrial Psychology

AIQ directly addresses a gap that industrial and organisational psychology is increasingly being asked to fill: how do we assess human judgment in the context of algorithmic decision support? Candidates with high AIQ scores are positioned to use AI as a responsible augmentation of human judgment rather than a replacement for it. This is particularly critical in people management, clinical, legal, and policy roles.

### Format

Scenario-based ethical reasoning assessment. Candidates evaluate AI-assisted decision scenarios and explain their reasoning. Duration: approximately 14–20 minutes.

### Outputs

- AIQ score and percentile ranking
- Breakdown across the five dimensions
- Role-specific ethical risk commentary
- Full PDF report

## Team Diagnostic: Ecosystem Index

### What It Is

The Ecosystem Index is a team-level diagnostic that moves beyond individual assessment to analyse the collective capability profile of an existing team — and uses that analysis to inform hiring decisions and team development strategy.

Rather than treating each hire as an isolated decision, the Ecosystem Index treats the team as a system and asks: *given who we already are, who do we need to become?*

### How It Works

**Step 1 — Team Assessment**
Existing team members complete the FIT Assessment. This produces individual behavioural profiles across the six core capabilities.

**Step 2 — Mission and Context Input**
The team lead or HR partner provides context about the organisation's mission, current challenges, planned growth, and areas of friction or underperformance.

**Step 3 — AI Analysis**
The platform analyses the team's collective capability profile against the stated mission and context, identifying:

- Where the team is strong relative to mission requirements
- Where capability gaps exist and how significant they are
- Which gaps are most likely creating the friction, delays, or risks described in the context input

**Step 4 — Outputs**

- **Capability Gap Matrix** — a visual breakdown of where the team sits versus where it needs to be across all six FIT dimensions
- **Mission Alignment Score** — how well the current team profile maps to the stated mission requirements
- **Contextual Insights** — AI-generated analysis connecting the team's capability gaps to the specific challenges described by the team lead
- **Ideal Candidate Profile** — a data-driven specification of the behavioural profile most needed in the next hire, with target score ranges across each FIT dimension
- **Risk Alerts** — flags for critical gaps that pose strategic risk if left unaddressed

### Why It Matters

Most hiring decisions are made without a systematic view of what the existing team lacks. The result is that organisations hire for individual excellence rather than collective fit — and teams that look strong on paper continue to underperform because of structural gaps no single hire has been selected to address.

The Ecosystem Index reframes hiring as a systems decision. It provides HR teams and business leaders with a scientifically grounded, data-driven basis for prioritising where to hire and what to look for.

## Relevance to Industrial and Organisational Psychology

### Standardisation and Consistency

Every candidate is assessed on the same dimensions, presented with equivalent stimuli, and scored against the same criteria. This eliminates the variability that characterises unstructured interviews and subjective CV review — the two most common sources of inconsistency in hiring.

### Bias Reduction

Because HumanMetrix assessments evaluate behavioural and cognitive responses rather than background, credentials, or self-presentation, they reduce the surface area for demographic bias. Candidates are evaluated on what they do, not who they appear to be.

### Transparency

Candidates receive their full results and can review the dimensions on which they were assessed. There are no hidden scores or opaque algorithms determining outcomes — every dimension assessed is named, defined, and explained in the candidate report.

### AI-Era Validity

The FIT competency framework and the AIQ assessment were developed specifically to address the competency gap in traditional psychometric tools — none of which were designed to evaluate human-AI collaboration, algorithmic reasoning, or the ethical judgment required in AI-augmented roles. HumanMetrix fills this gap with purpose-built assessments grounded in behavioural science.
